Akili Ecomuseum Network

Launch Date: October 6, 2026 Founding Director: Abdul Mubarak Rick Permanent DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23180750

Institutional Mission

The Akili Ecomuseum Network (AEN) is a decentralized, open-access digital public good archiving ecological health, indigenous heritage, oral histories, and environmental stewardship across East Africa's continuous hydrological corridors.

By connecting mountain glaciers, riverine basins, estuarine deltas, and coastal marine sanctuaries into a unified digital ecosystem, AEN bridges freshwater science, Swahili maritime culture, and community-led conservation.

Highland-to-Ocean Node Network

The network operates through an open, non-ownership digital node model spanning an 800-kilometer hydrological corridor across Kenya:

Node 00 • Highland Headwater

Mount Kenya Water Tower

Glacial catchments, cloud forests, and sacred mountain hydrology feeding the Tana and Athi river basins.

Node 01 • Savanna Hydrology

Mara River Basin

Transboundary riverine dynamics, pastoralist land management, and aquatic wildlife corridor documentation.

Node 02 • Highland-to-Reef River

Athi-Galana-Sabaki Basin

A 390-km river corridor tracking siltation, riverine ecology, and freshwater flow into the Sabaki Estuary.

Node 03 • Riverine Delta

Tana River Basin & Delta

Kenya's largest deltaic ecosystem, oxbow wetlands, Pokomo riverine farming heritage, and mangrove nurseries.

Node 04 • Subterranean & Reef

Shimoni Caves & Kisite

Coral rag hydrology, subterranean caves, Kisite-Mpunguti marine sanctuaries, and dolphin habitats.

Node 05 • Coastal Rainforest

Shimba Hills Ridge

Rainforest headwaters, endemic species corridors, and sacred Mijikenda Kaya forest stewardship.

Node 06 • Estuarine Port

Mombasa & Tudor Creek

Urban mangrove restoration, creek hydrology, Old Town dhow harbors, and Swahili maritime trade history.

Node 07 • Marine Sanctuary

Kilifi & Watamu Reefs

Coral lagoons, sea turtle nesting beaches, Mida Creek mangroves, and BMU community governance.

Node 08 • Maritime Archipelago

Lamu & Bajuni Heritage

UNESCO Swahili stone architecture, dhow building craftsmanship, seagrass beds, and mangrove conservation.

Governance & Open Access

All digital archives, educational modules, and geospatial records published by the Akili Ecomuseum Network are held in digital public trust and licensed under Creative Commons CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Indigenous oral histories and traditional ecological knowledge remain the perpetual cultural property of their contributing communities.
