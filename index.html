Institutional Mission

The Akili Ecomuseum Network (AEN) is a decentralized, open-access digital public good archiving ecological health, indigenous heritage, oral histories, and environmental stewardship across East Africa's continuous hydrological corridors.

By connecting mountain glaciers, riverine basins, estuarine deltas, and coastal marine sanctuaries into a unified digital ecosystem, AEN bridges freshwater science, Swahili maritime culture, and community-led conservation.

Highland-to-Ocean Node Network Connecting JSON API...

The network operates through an open, non-ownership digital node model spanning an 800-kilometer hydrological corridor across Kenya. Live machine-readable data is served directly via nodes.json.

Governance & Open Access

All digital archives, educational modules, and geospatial records published by the Akili Ecomuseum Network are held in digital public trust and licensed under Creative Commons CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Indigenous oral histories and traditional ecological knowledge remain the perpetual cultural property of their contributing communities.

To submit field notes or research contributions, view our Contributing Guidelines.
